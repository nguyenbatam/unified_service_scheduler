# Testing: stress, bench and chaos

How the running cluster is checked beyond the Go test suite (unit and integration tests: [README → Test](../README.md#test)).

## Stress: the requirements matrix

Go client (`backend/cmd/stress`) against the running cluster. Every phase gathers evidence for a requirement of the brief, and the run ends with a matrix — each requirement `PASS`/`FAIL`/`SKIP` with the evidence behind it (JSON with `REPORT_JSON=path`):

| Requirement | Evidence the run gathers |
|---|---|
| R1 book a vehicle + service(s) + dealership at a time | every phase books single and multi-line jobs, as staff and as signed-in customers |
| R2a bay AND qualified technician for the **entire** duration | SQL pass over the whole database: no bay/technician/vehicle overlap, every technician qualified and on duty for the whole window, every bay of the right type; starts free at their beginning but not for the whole job are refused |
| R2b real-time availability | **truth**: one day filled booking by booking — every slot (and its free bay/technician counts) equals an oracle built from the raw tables with the documented rules re-stated in the client (job lines, soft upgrade, express spill, one-move repair, shifts, breaks, absences), every offered start books on the *other* backend, the other backend shows the new counts right after, starts reported full are refused (a probe left without a definite answer is counted apart, never as an overbooking), and once nothing is offered the oracle agrees nothing fits; then bookings are cancelled and rescheduled on one backend and every change is re-read on the other against the oracle: a cancelled start must be offered again (`truth churn`); **hammer/fill/customers**: every 409/422 is timestamped and judged against the holds *when it was decided*, rebuilt from `appointment_history` (bookings whose TX began after the answer left out, every position a booking held while the request could have run kept, so it can only hide a false refusal): no refusal while a bay + on-duty technician — free, or freed by moving one confirmed booking —, staging room and the car were free; the fill demand's refusals are judged right after the demand, before the top-up fills the day; a phase with no refusal records SKIP, not PASS; the fill day is topped up until nothing is offered and the oracle must agree it is full |
| R3 confirmed record (customer, vehicle, technician, bay) | every booking clients were told about is a row; read back through both backends; the customer owns the car; snapshot consistent |
| Q1 concurrency + idempotency | `CONCURRENCY` requests per contended slot (default 1000 × `SLOTS` 5; check-all and the Cluster workflow run 300 × 3), a quarter of them waiters (on Northside, whose express limit the run raises to 120 min so its 90/120-min general jobs qualify, the soft upgrade is raced too): exactly the capacity wins (counted in SQL even when answers were lost); **storm**: reschedules (If-Match) and new bookings released together for one start — exactly its room ends there, refused reschedules stay put at their version; one appointment per Idempotency-Key, raced on both backends at once over several keys; with `MIXED_OPS` > 0 (30 on a full `stress.sh` run and in cluster-checks, 0 when phases are picked), reassign / reschedule / cancel with If-Match mid-hammer, each applied at least once, stale versions 412, plus a burst of back-to-back reassigns of one booking and concurrent reschedules on one version (one winner, the rest 412) |
| Q2 scalability | both backends answer writes; same record everywhere |
| Q3 performance | fill day (open-loop arrivals, nobody waits for the generator by default — `FILL_WORKERS` 0 = all): p99 booking / availability from each actual send within `SLO_BOOK_MS` / `SLO_READ_MS`, and the first read timed from the customer's *intended* arrival within `SLO_READ_MS` (no coordinated omission); hammered bookings (`SLO_HAMMER_MS`, 3 s ≈ 3× a laptop's p99) and dispatcher reads (`SLO_SIDE_MS`); bookings on **another day** of the hammered dealership during the hammer (`SLO_OTHER_DAY_MS`: other days stay parallel); the DB pools never reach their cap during a hammer (most scrapes must succeed; `POOL_CAP_FAILS=false` only reports it) and add at most `POOL_WAIT_BUDGET_MS` of empty-acquire wait. The Cluster workflow sets runner-sized budgets (`.github/workflows/cluster.yml`) |
| Q4 reliability | outbox fully published, every event (created, moved, reassigned, cancelled…) reaches every WebSocket listener on both backends, and outside chaos no listener may lose its session (a drop misses events) — except the hub's designed eviction of a slow consumer (close 4000 "slow consumer"), reported as INFO with its count; per appointment, versions arrive in order on every listener (duplicates reported) — judged only when appointments changed while listened, i.e. with `MIXED_OPS` > 0, else that check is SKIP; reads stay 2xx under load |
| Q5 optimisation | judged right after the fill day's demand (before the top-up fills what a misplaced job should have used): no job — waiter or not, both get the same best-fit technician order — on a more senior technician than a free qualified one, no waiter job in the quick lane while a general bay is free, no express job spilled into a general bay while an express bay is free ("free" = no hold had it over the job's window at any time since the demand began, from `appointment_history`: a one-move repair can free a resource that was busy when a job was placed); the booked labour minutes stay under an offline capacity bound (reported as a %), within `FILL_MARGIN_PCT` (10 %) of the same demand replayed one customer at a time, and that replay books at least as much as (`FIRSTFIT_MARGIN_PCT`, default 0: never fewer) the same demand placed offline by a naive first-fit allocator on the oracle's model of the shop in the workshop's listed resource order — a lower bound, not only a ceiling; the fill demand asks ~4.6× what the day holds, so every greedy allocator saturates it and the gain there is ~0 by construction; **fit days** (`Q5/fit gain`): the same mixes and wishes sized to 1.0 / 1.15 / 1.3 × the capacity bound (where placement decides what still fits), booked one at a time, each day no worse than first fit in the listed order (quick lane and junior technicians first) and together at least `FIT_MIN_GAIN_PCT` (0) above first fit in the median of 24 random resource orders — the Q5 headline; measured against the engine over 20 seeds × 2 rosters: +5.6 % (weekday) / +3.6 % (Saturday) over the median order pooled (seed 1, the default: +4.2 % / +2.6 %), but only +0.0 … +0.3 % over the listed order (on random demand a sensible first fit is most of best fit; `TestFitDaysGainOverFirstFit` pins ≥ +2 % pooled); **quality**: a scripted day ("Stress Quality", 11 requests at exact starts, one at a time) where greedy first fit provably loses — best fit keeping a stretch whole, a waiter's job moved into the quick lane, an oil change moved to the other senior technician — is replayed offline by first fit in *every* fixed order of the bays and technicians (naive and own-bay-type-first, 1440 replays): they book 600 … 780 of the 960 labour minutes asked, the server must book `QUALITY_MARGIN_PCT` (15 %) more than the best of them (it books all 960, +23.1 %, = the offline optimum), with at least one one-move repair attributed from `appointment_history` to the request that needed it (`Q5/beats first fit`, `Q5/repair`) and the SQL integrity pass clean over the day |
| Q6 observability | Σ `scheduler_bookings_total{result=created}` across instances = rows created |
| Q7 many users | N signed-in customers (provisioned accounts) book their own cars at once; nobody can read another's appointment or book another's car |

```bash
./scripts/stress.sh                       # all phases: river,north (hammer) storm truth fill quality customers
PHASES=truth,fill ./scripts/stress.sh     # one at a time
CONCURRENCY=10000 SLOTS=5 WORKERS=1024 ./scripts/stress.sh
SOAK_DURATION=2h ./scripts/soak.sh        # steady mixed load: queues, memory and goroutines must stay flat (refreshes its session)
```

**Which checks carry the proof.** The verdicts that could catch a wrong server are the ones computed independently of it: the SQL integrity pass (which fails over a run with no rows instead of passing) and the oracle (raw rows + the rules — calendar included: clocks, the slot grid, staging windows — re-stated in `cmd/stress/rules.go` and `repair.go`, never the engine's code), the refusal judgement against the holds when each refusal was decided, the storm and river counts in Postgres, the first-fit replays (every resource order on the quality day), the exhaustive optimum and the capacity bounds, and the metrics reconciliation. Some checks are defence in depth only: `overlapSQL` (and the client's board overlap check) cannot fire while the GiST constraints exist. A hammered slot's winner bound comes from the oracle's model of the workshop (`cmd/stress/bound.go`), not from the server's availability, and the same oracle is checked against `booking.Service.Availability` on every PR (`TestOracleAgreesWithAvailability`, and `TestOracleAgreesWithAvailabilityAcrossZones` in Sydney and on New York's clock-change days).

The truth/fill/customers phases run on their own dealership ("Stress Workshop": 7 bays, 6 technicians with overlapping skills, two shift patterns with breaks and a dated absence on every phase day), the quality day on a third ("Stress Quality": an express and two general bays, three brake technicians on duty 07:00–14:00 and two senior ones 14:00–19:00, so the script's blocks never interact), and river on another ("Stress River", no staging cap, so its exact check can never silently degrade), all created through the admin API on first use; the customer accounts are provisioned in Postgres and signed with the server's `JWT_SECRET` (a single load generator would otherwise hit the per-IP register/login limits), and re-signed before each token expires. Signed-in users (admin, stress owner, demo customer) each keep one session with its own cookie jar, shared by every client acting as that user: the access token is refreshed through `POST /api/v1/auth/refresh` at 80 % of its lifetime (one refresh per token however many goroutines need it; a 401 renews it once and resends only reads, PUTs and writes carrying an Idempotency-Key or If-Match; a refresh whose answer was lost signs in afresh rather than replay a possibly rotated cookie), and WS listeners redial with the current token. A session that cannot be renewed aborts the run as a client failure (never a 409/422 or a missing booking); `Q4/client session` PASSes when the run outlasted the access TTL and refreshed (INFO on a shorter run).

## Requirements matrix: tests and thresholds

For each requirement of the brief (R1–R3) and each quality goal (Q1–Q7): the integration tests that pin it on every PR, the stress phase that checks it on the running cluster, and the threshold.

`scripts/stress.sh` ends with a requirements matrix — first one line per requirement (verdict, a headline number, the names of skipped checks), then every piece of evidence; JSON with `REPORT_JSON`, a Markdown copy with `STRESS_SUMMARY_MD` (the Cluster workflow's job summary). A row passes on any passing evidence and fails on any failing one; INFO lines are facts (ratios, distributions) that never pass a row, and a check that had nothing to judge (no refusal happened, no row of the run in Postgres) records SKIP or FAIL, never PASS. `STRESS_REQUIRE` lists what must PASS (a SKIP fails): row ids, one named check (`Q4/ws order`), or `all` (every row plus `Q4/ws`, `Q4/ws order`, `Q5/beats first fit`, `Q5/repair` and `Q5/fit gain`; the default of a full run). The oracle (`cmd/stress`: `oracle.go`, `repair.go`, `rules.go`) reads only raw rows and restates the documented rules itself (job lines, soft upgrade, express spill, the one-move repair, roster duty with shifts, breaks and absences, a flagged overrun holding its bay and technician until it is completed) and the calendar (clock parsing, the 30-minute grid stepped in elapsed time, local instants via `time.Date`, staging windows), never importing the server's (`booking.ParseClock`, `Clock.On`, `SlotStep`, `ArrivalWindow` included); CI runs it against the engine's availability on every PR (`TestOracleAgreesWithAvailability`; `TestOracleAgreesWithAvailabilityAcrossZones` in Sydney summer time, where every morning start is on the previous UTC date, and in New York on both clock-change Sundays with the shop open across the change; `TestOracleStartsOnClockChangeDays` pins the start count and distinct instants of those days).

| Req | Integration tests (`internal/integration`, CI) | Threshold (stress run; evidence per phase in the table above) |
|---|---|---|
| R1 book | `TestMultiLineAppointment`, `TestBookAssignsCustomerFromVehicle` | > 0 bookings; 0 5xx/400/unreadable |
| R2a whole duration, never double-booked | `TestExclusionConstraints`, `TestBookingRefusedWhenResourceBusyOnlyInSecondHalf`, `TestOneBayTwoQualifiedTechniciansOneWinner`, `TestStagingCapacityHoldsUnderConcurrency` | 0 accepted; 0 offending rows |
| R2b real-time availability | self-consistency (the engine's availability vs its own booking, so a rule both get wrong passes): `TestAvailabilityMatchesBookingForEverySlot`, `TestAvailabilityParityRandomWorkshops`, `TestAvailabilityParityRepairHeavy`; the independent check: `cmd/stress` `TestOracleAgreesWithAvailability` (raw rows + restated rules vs availability) | 0 mismatches, 0 false offers, 0 false refusals, 0 cancelled starts still full |
| R3 record persisted | `TestCreatedEventPayloadMatchesAppointment`, `TestBookedSnapshotSurvivesCatalogChange` | 0 missing, 0 diffs |
| Q1 concurrency, idempotency | `TestSlotBurst`, `TestConcurrentBookingsFillEachBayOnce`, `TestRepairRaceOneWinner`, `TestConcurrentRetrySameKeyReplays` | exact counts; 1 appointment per key; 412 on stale versions |
| Q2 stateless replicas | `TestSlotBurst` (two instances), `TestHTTPUserCacheLogoutAndInvalidate` | ≥ `MIN_INSTANCES` (2) instances answered |
| Q3 performance | `TestBookingWritesNeverWaitForASecondPoolConnection`, `TestDayLockQueuesOnlyThatDay` | p99 hammer 3 s, side 1 s, other day 1 s, fill booking 1 s / read 500 ms; Σ empty-acquire wait ≤ 2 s per phase |
| Q4 reliability | `TestOutboxConcurrentWorkersKeepAggregateOrder`, `TestOutboxRetryBackoffAndPoison`, `TestBookingSurvivesRedisOutage` | 0 pending/failed rows, 0 gaps, 0 reordered, 0 5xx |
| Q5 optimisation | `TestAllocationRegretNoWorseThanFirstFit`, `TestTechnicianBestFitKeepsLongStretch`, `TestWaiterSoftUpgradeRacingOilChange`, `TestExpressSpillsToGeneralOnlyWhenTheQuickLaneIsTaken` | 0 misplaced; ≤ 10 % below the replay (`FILL_MARGIN_PCT`); never below first fit in the listed order (`FIRSTFIT_MARGIN_PCT`, default 0; fill and each fit day); fit days ≥ the median first-fit order (`FIT_MIN_GAIN_PCT`, default 0; the DB test pins ≥ +2 % pooled); quality ≥ +15 % over the best first fit (`QUALITY_MARGIN_PCT`; one lost job of the script is +11.5 %) and ≥ 1 repair; 0 wrong move counts |
| Q6 observability | `TestMetricsExportPoolAndInFlight` | exact |
| Q7 many users | `TestCustomerSeesAndActsOnlyOnOwnData`, `TestHTTPCustomerSelfServiceFlow` | 0 leaks, 0 ownership mismatches |

`scripts/soak.sh` runs a steady mixed load for hours and fails if the outbox/notification backlog, resident memory or goroutines keep growing. `scripts/bench.sh` ramps open-loop load per workload and prints, per step, client latency and dispatch lag next to server throughput, server mean latency, in-flight vs λ·W, pool use, and Postgres commits per request, with the host context (CPU, RAM, Docker, pool sizes); hot rounds are counted in Postgres (exactly 20 per start), the journey books as signed-in customers, wrong answers (a hot round over capacity or short while requests were refused, an uncontended booking refused) fail the run whatever the load, and the SQL integrity pass runs over everything it booked.

## Bench: capacity on this machine

```bash
./scripts/bench.sh    # book (uncontended), read (the days book filled), hot (one start), journey (signed-in customers)
```

Each workload offers an increasing open-loop rate and stops at the first step that fails: p99 over budget, more than 1 % errors / busy / 429 / client-dropped, under 90 % of the offered rate achieved, a hot round not holding exactly its places, an uncontended booking refused, or client dispatch lag showing the generator, not the stack, fell behind. Wrong answers (a hot round over capacity or short while requests were refused, an uncontended `book`/`journey` booking refused) also fail the run, whatever the load; every step prints the client view next to the server view (throughput, mean latency, in-flight vs λ·W, pool use, Postgres commits per request) and the run starts with the host context. Hot rounds are counted in Postgres (each fully sent round holds exactly its 20 places), and the run ends with the SQL integrity pass over everything it booked and the outbox drained. The journey books as signed-in customers (customer policy and throttle on the path): from one machine its knee is the per-IP customer budget (120 bookings / 600 reads a minute by default), so the stack's write capacity is the `book` row — or raise `WRITE_LIMIT_PER_IP` / `AVAILABILITY_LIMIT_PER_IP` on the stack to bench the customer path itself.

Example — Apple M4 (10 cores, 16 GiB), Docker Desktop, 2 backends × 20 pool connections, Postgres 18, 10 s steps, fresh database, re-run on 18 Oct 2026 with an IDE open and the host swapping (3.2 of 4 GB swap in use), so read these as a floor, not a ceiling. The journey ran with `WRITE_LIMIT_PER_IP` / `AVAILABILITY_LIMIT_PER_IP` raised.

| Workload | Sustained (p99 within budget) | p99 there | Next step | What saturates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| read (availability of a day) | **3200 req/s** | 40 ms | 6400 offered → 4423 served, p99 1.3 s | pool 38/40 |
| book (uncontended) | **200 bookings/s** | 34 ms | 400 offered → 400 served, p99 647 ms (budget 500) | waits for a pool connection |
| hot (100 racers per start, 20 places) | **100 req/s** in one run; the next run missed it (67 served, p99 5.3 s) | 200 ms | 400 offered → 185 served, p99 13.7 s | the start's day-lock queue; pool 10–16/40 with no acquire wait |
| journey (signed-in customers: availability + booking) | **16 users/s** (the highest default step; 32 customers × 30 writes a minute per user) | 16 ms | not reached | — |

Every row's correctness checks passed: no bay, technician or vehicle overlap, each hot round held exactly its 20 places, the outbox drained. An earlier run on the same laptop reported 800 bookings/s and 800 hot req/s; the re-run did not reproduce that, so only the re-run is shown.

How the numbers relate: at every knee the in-flight gauge matches Little's law (in-flight ≈ server throughput × server mean latency, ratio 1.00–1.04), so the latency is queueing, not slow SQL. For read and book it is the wait for a pool connection (the empty-acquire wait climbs while server mean latency jumps from ~2 ms to ~100 ms for book and ~850 ms for read); raising `DB_MAX_CONNS` (and `max_connections`) is the first lever. For hot it is the queue for that start's dealership-day lock while the pool stays free: every booking of one dealership-day commits in turn, so that one day's throughput follows commit latency, which is why it swings most on a loaded host. The knees are this laptop's, not the design's: each dealership-day is independent, so bookings scale out across days, dealerships, and backends.

## Chaos

`./scripts/cluster-checks.sh chaos` (part of `check-all.sh`): with Redis stopped a small river hammer with If-Match mutations, the customers phase and the cross-instance idempotency race still pass (the truth phase runs only in the full stress run) (idempotency on Postgres, rate limits fail open); with Kafka paused bookings still commit, their events queue in the outbox, the backlog drains once Kafka is back, and the events reach WebSocket listeners on both backends that stayed connected through the pause; with backend-1 SIGKILLed mid-hammer (the hammer is sent straight to it, so the kill always cuts requests off) and restarted, clients retry with their keys, every request the kill cut off is replayed — at least one is required; unresolved ones must now be definite, ones answered on a retry must replay consistently — and created ones replay 200 with the same id, and river's exact capacity, the refusal oracle, SQL integrity and the outbox drain all still hold. Each chaos stress run writes its matrix JSON to `CHAOS_REPORT_DIR` (a CI artifact).
